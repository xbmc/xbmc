01What this is for

A widescreen film is usually delivered inside a 16:9 frame with black bars baked into the picture. Kodi has never known they were there. It knows the shape of the frame, and it treats that as the shape of the film.

On a television that hardly matters — the bars are black, the screen is black, nobody looks. In a room with a masked screen or an anamorphic lens it matters a great deal, because something has to know how tall the picture actually is before it can drive a mask to the edge of it. Today that job is done by external video processors that re-measure the picture after Kodi has already drawn it.

This feature moves that knowledge inside Kodi. It measures the picture area of each video, stores it in the library, resolves it against a vocabulary of real aspect ratios, and then publishes it — to skins, to add-ons, and over JSON-RPC — alongside the shape of the screen the viewer says they have.

The four rooms

The design is shaped by four kinds of viewing setup, in increasing order of what they need:

  1. A television. The screen is the whole picture. Nothing here needs to change, and by default nothing does.
  2. A fixed screen, projector zoomed by hand. The screen is a shape the display is not. Kodi needs to be told that shape and stay inside it.
  3. Two-way masking. Something must be told how tall the picture is, per title, before the film starts.
  4. Motorised lens and masking. The room's shape changes per title, so the area Kodi operates in is not a setting at all — it is something an automation states, and Kodi follows.
Default behaviour

Every setting in this feature is off, or set to a value that is inert, out of the box. Screen aspect ratio defaults to Same as the display, which is drawing-inert by construction on every display, window and calibration. A viewer who never opens this page sees byte-identical behaviour to Kodi today.

02The four ideas

Four things get confused with one another constantly in this area, and the whole feature falls out of keeping them apart.

The frame

What is coded in the file — 1920×1080, corrected for pixel aspect. This is what Kodi has always reported as the aspect ratio, and it is what the skin's existing VideoPlayer.VideoAspect still reports.

The content geometry

The lit rectangle inside the frame, with the coded black bars excluded. For a scope film in a 16:9 container this is roughly 1920×800 — the thing a mask is driven to. It is discovered by measurement, not by parsing metadata, because no metadata records it.

The raster

The area Kodi may operate in — the shape of the physical screen. This is the load-bearing invariant of the whole feature:

The invariant

Nothing Kodi draws ever exceeds the raster. Not the picture, not the OSD, not a dialogue, not a subtitle, not the mouse pointer.

That single rule dissolves several settings that otherwise look necessary. At rest the OSD has only one place to be. The picture is always contained. A motorised room does not opt out of the constraint — it moves it.

The picture as drawn

Where the content actually landed on the screen, in screen pixels, after the raster, the calibration and any maintained ratio have all had their say. Neither the content's ratio nor the raster's ratio tells you this; it has to be observed from what the renderer drew, and it is reported separately.

Why some ratios cannot be measured into

Four entries carry detect="false". They are ratios rare enough that a measurement landing on one is more likely to be an overshoot on a neighbour — and getting that wrong reports a picture narrower than the truth, which crops it. A square picture is what a failed measurement looks like, not a ratio anyone delivers, so 1.00 is declare-only too.

Tolerance, and exact fractions

A measurement within 2% of an entry is called that entry. The allowance exists because real encodes are not exact: 1888×1080 is 1.748, which is a correct 16:9 encode and 1.8% away from nominal. The 4:3 and 16:9 entries are stored as exact fractions (1.3333333, 1.7777778) so that a full-frame title resolves to exactly its own frame rather than a rectangle a few pixels short of it.

Direction of resolution

A measurement can sit within tolerance of two entries at once — 3840×1608 is 2.3881, which is legitimately close to both 2.35 and 2.40. Kodi breaks the tie toward the shape the room is resting at: in a 2.40 room it resolves to 2.40 and fills the screen; in a 1.78 room it resolves to 2.35. Both answers are correct; the one that costs the viewer least is chosen.

Overriding the vocabulary

Copy system/aspectratios.xml to your userdata folder and edit the copy. It is applied over the shipped file rather than instead of it, so it only needs the ratios you want to change, and you still receive any ratio a later Kodi adds. Entries match at two decimal places. 

<!-- userdata/aspectratios.xml -->
<aspectratios>
  <ratio value="1.50" detect="true"/>
  <ratio value="1.66" name="European widescreen"/>
</aspectratios>

Attributes you leave out keep whatever the entry already had, so you can change one property on its own. The name is never translated — it is the same string in every language, so anything reading it can compare against it.

04Measuring content

With Detect content aspect ratio on, Kodi samples each video at a number of points across its runtime, decodes a frame at each, and finds the lit rectangle within it. The result is stored against the file in the video library, so it is measured once and read thereafter.

What gets excluded, and why

The first and last minutes of a title are skipped by default. This is not a performance trick — it is accuracy. Studio logos and service idents are frequently a different shape from the film, and measuring one is exactly how a widescreen film gets recorded as full frame. A credit roll is frequently full frame on a widescreen title, and credits over black describe the text rather than any picture.

Titles that change shape partway through

A film with sequences shot for IMAX is genuinely two shapes. Kodi records every distinct geometry it found as a section, dominant first, and flags the title as varying. What it then serves depends on Variable aspect ratio content:

  • Use envelope ratio + bars — report an area large enough for every part of the picture. Nothing is ever cropped; the narrower sequences show bars.
  • Use dominant ratio + clip — report the shape the title is mostly in, so everything can be masked or zoomed to it, at the cost of losing the edges of the other sequences.

A second shape has to occupy a meaningful share of the samples before the title counts as varying. That threshold is deliberately not a user-facing setting — see advancedsettings.xml.

Where an answer came from

Every surface that reports geometry also reports its source, because acting on a guess and acting on a measurement are different decisions:

sourceMeaning
containerNothing is known. The coded frame is being served as the answer.
cachedA stored measurement from the library.
liveRead from the frames currently being decoded.
declaredStated by hand or by an automation, overriding detection.
rejected

A measurement can exist and be refused by the plausibility gate, in which case the frame is served instead. That is otherwise indistinguishable from never having measured at all — both report container, both serve the frame. The rejected flag is the only thing that tells them apart, and which one it is decides whether the answer is to measure the file or to look at why detection failed on it.

What can and cannot be measured

Local and network files only. Discs and disc images cannot be sampled. Sampling costs roughly half a second per point for an episode and a second for a film, which is why the whole feature is off by default and why measuring during a library scan is a separate opt-in — it turns a scan of a large library from minutes into hours.

05Live detection during playback

Scan-time measurement knows a title changes shape, but not when. Live detection reads every frame as it plays and fits the picture to the shape actually on screen, so a title that changes shape partway through follows it from the frame it changes on.

The two work together rather than competing: what was measured before playback still decides how far the picture may open, and live detection only moves it within that. The scan owns what is stored; live detection is serve-only and never writes back.

Asymmetric confirmation

Growing and shrinking are not the same risk, and are not treated the same way. A larger picture is reported on the frame it appears, because nothing is at risk. A smaller one must persist for a few frames first, because reporting it wrongly crops real picture — and a shot that happens to be dark along its edges reads smaller than it is. Setting the confirmation window to one frame reports both immediately.

Constraint

Live detection reads frames from software-decoded local and network sources only. Turn it off if a video processor already tracks the picture in real time, or if you would rather the picture never changed size mid-film.

06Settings reference

Everything lives on one page — Settings › System › Screen & Aspect Ratio — in four groups. Splitting one feature across several pages costs more than the taxonomy buys, so some of it deliberately crosses into playback territory.

Group 1 — The Screen

Advanced videoscreen.screenalignment action

Screen alignment

Shows a frame at each aspect ratio you choose, drawn at the true shape and at the full size of the display, so you can set a projector's position, zoom and lens shift against the shape of your screen. Align optically first: nothing here is corrected or scaled, and a picture that lands where it should needs no correction afterwards.

Placed first in the group on purpose — a viewer setting their screen to 2.40 is exactly the viewer who needs a true 2.40 frame to align against, and should not have to find it on another page. See Screen alignment.

Advanced videoscreen.rasteraspect default Same as the display

Screen aspect ratio

The shape of the screen the picture is projected onto. Kodi keeps everything it draws inside that shape, so a projector zoomed to fill a wider screen no longer puts menus and subtitles past the edges. Leave this at Same as the display for a television or a monitor, where the screen is the whole picture.

Options are the detectable entries of the aspect ratio vocabulary, plus Same as the display, which is drawing-inert and is the default. Changing this reloads the skin so it can re-select a layout for the new shape.

Group 2 — The Interface

Advanced videoscreen.guikeepshape default Off

Maintain original OSD aspect ratio

Draws the OSD and menus at the shape the skin's layout was drawn at, centred, instead of stretching that layout to fill a screen of a different shape. Kodi already picks the closest layout a skin offers, so a skin with one near the shape of your screen fills it either way — this matters most for a skin that has only one.

The shape held is that of the layout Kodi selected, not 16:9. A skin may declare several resolution variants and Kodi picks the one closest to the raster, so on a 2.40 screen Estuary is already being drawn at 21:9 before this setting is consulted at all.

There are three outcomes, not two:

  • Within 2%The selected variant is close enough to the area's shape that it is stretched to fill it. No bars, no surround.
  • Beyond 2%The variant is held at its own shape and centred. What is left over is the surround.
  • Setting offAlways stretched to fill, however far off the shape is.

The tolerance is the aspect ratio vocabulary's own, judged as |ln(have / wanted)| ≤ 0.02. It exists because selection picks the closest declared variant, so the two are routinely a fraction of a percent apart — and holding on that difference would put a permanent few-pixel bar around the interface for no visible gain.

This governs menus only. It is suspended during fullscreen video, where the OSD spans the raster.

With Estuary, this often does nothing

Estuary declares eight variants, densely covering the wide range, so most rasters land inside the tolerance and are filled rather than held — there is nothing left over to surround. It bites at 2.76, where nothing that wide is declared, and at 1.85, where 17:9 is 1.8889 and misses the threshold by a hair (0.0208 against 0.0200), giving a ~9px band. The real audience for this setting is a skin with a single layout.

Advanced videoscreen.guisurround default Skin default

Fill the unused area around the OSD with

What is drawn in the part of the screen the OSD leaves uncovered while it keeps its original aspect ratio. A backdrop that fades toward the edges can read better than a hard black border.

  • Skin defaultWhatever the skin itself asks for, or black when it asks for nothing.
  • A colourSet by videoscreen.guisurroundcolour, default a dark grey FF1A1A1A.
  • An imageSet by videoscreen.guisurroundimage. Fitted at its own aspect ratio, never stretched.

Only enabled while Maintain original OSD aspect ratio is on. A skin can declare its own surround — see For skinners.

Group 3 — During Playback

Advanced videoscreen.osdplaying default The whole screen

Lay the OSD out against

Where the on-screen display, menus and dialogues are laid out while a video is playing or paused. The picture keeps them inside the picture as it is drawn, so on a masked screen or with an anamorphic lens nothing lands on the mask. On an ordinary screen the black bars are visible and are usable space, so leave this at The whole screen.

The video itself is never moved or resized by this setting.

Advanced videoscreen.variablecontentgeometry default Use envelope ratio + bars

Variable aspect ratio content

What to report for a title whose picture changes shape partway through, such as a film with sequences shot for IMAX.

  • Use envelope ratio + barsReports an area large enough for every part of the picture, leaving black bars visible during the narrower sequences.
  • Use dominant ratio + clipReports the shape the title is mostly in, so everything can be cropped or zoomed to it, at the cost of losing the edges of the other sequences.

Group 4 — Detection

Advanced videoscreen.extractcontentgeometry default Off

Detect content aspect ratio

Measure the picture area of each video ahead of playback, so that black bars coded into the file are known rather than guessed and can be followed by screen masking or projector lens control. Sampling a file takes several seconds, so this is off by default. Local and network files only; discs and disc images cannot be sampled.

This is the master switch for the group — everything below is disabled until it is on.

Advanced videoscreen.contentgeometryonscan default Off

Measure while scanning the library

Measure each item as the library scan adds it, rather than leaving it to the background scan afterwards. Every item is then complete as soon as it appears, but a scan of a large library takes hours instead of minutes.

Advanced videoscreen.contentgeometrysamples default 9 · 5–45

Samples per video

How many points across each video to measure. Deciding that a title changes shape partway through depends on catching more than one of its sections, so more samples find more of them, at the cost of a slower scan. Sampling one point takes around half a second for an episode and around a second for a film.

Expert videoscreen.contentgeometryleadin default 2 min · 0–15

Ignore at the start of each video

Runtime to leave unmeasured at the beginning of each video. Studio logos and service idents are often a different shape from the title itself, and measuring one is how a widescreen film gets recorded as full frame.

Expert videoscreen.contentgeometryleadout default 1 min · 0–15

Ignore at the end of each video

Runtime to leave unmeasured at the end of each video. A credit roll is frequently full frame on a widescreen title, and credits over black describe the text rather than any picture. Raise this for a library of films with long credits; lower it to measure titles whose closing minutes are genuinely a different shape.

Advanced videoscreen.livecontentgeometry default Off

Rescale the picture as its aspect ratio changes

Reads every frame while a file plays and fits the picture to the shape actually on screen, so a title that changes shape partway through follows it from the frame it changes on. What was measured before playback still decides how far the picture can open; this only moves it within that. Reads frames from software-decoded local and network sources only.

Expert videoscreen.livegeometrynarrow default 3 frames · 1–10

Confirm a smaller picture for

Frames a smaller picture must persist for before it is reported. A larger picture is reported on the frame it appears, because nothing is at risk; a smaller one crops real picture if it is wrong, and a shot that is dark along its edges reads smaller than it is. The next frame reports both immediately.

Expert videoscreen.livegeometryleadin default 1 min · 0–15

Ignore at the start of playback

Runtime at the beginning of a title during which live readings are not taken, judged on playback position. Idents and studio logos are often a different shape from the title, so the picture would be rescaled to a shape the film is not in before the film has begun. Sources with no duration have no position for the window to be relative to, and are not excluded.

Expert videoscreen.livegeometryleadout default 1 min · 0–15

Ignore at the end of playback

Runtime at the end of a title during which live readings are not taken. A credit roll is frequently full frame on a widescreen title, and rescaling the picture to it as the film ends is a change nothing on screen explains.

Advanced videoscreen.scancontentgeometry action

Scan library for content aspect ratios

Measure every video that has no measurement yet, including any that could not be read on a previous attempt. Runs in the background and pauses while anything is playing.

Elsewhere in Settings

Advanced subtitles.aligntocontent default Off

Position subtitles by the picture — Player › Subtitles

Applies to the "inside the video" alignments. Measures the inset from the picture rather than from the whole video, so that subtitles are not placed in a black bar the display crops away. Only enabled when subtitle alignment is set to one of the inside positions.

07What it switches off

Several existing settings assume Kodi does not know the shape of the room. Once a screen shape is stated, their premises no longer hold, and leaving them active would fight the raster rather than compose with it. They are therefore disabled — the stored choice is kept, so clearing the raster restores it.

Setting or featureWhat happens while a raster is stated
Stretch to minimise black bars
videoplayer.errorinaspect		 Disabled. The picture is fitted to its content and never stretched, so that it corresponds to a mask or a lens.
Zoom for 4:3 on widescreen
videoplayer.stretch43		 Disabled, for the same reason.
Computed view modes
Zoom, Wide zoom, Stretch, Original, 110%, 120%		 Withdrawn from the OSD list and the remote quick-cycle, and coerced to Normal. They compute from the coded frame while the crop draws content, so "zoom until no bars" over-zooms substantially on a bar-cut title.

Four playback controls remain valid under a raster, each defensible in room terms:

  • Zoom — lens driving.
  • Vertical shift — one-way masking.
  • Pixel ratio — repairing a broken encode.
  • The aspect ratio declaration — pinning a title whose measurement is wrong.
Why it is greyed rather than hidden

Greyed settings stay focusable and still show their help text, which is where the reason lives. Genuinely dynamic help would be new core work and was not attempted.

08Screen alignment

A separate full-screen tool that draws a frame at each aspect ratio you select, at the true shape and at the full size of the display. It is for setting a projector's position, zoom and lens shift against the physical screen — you align the optics to the frame, rather than correcting anything in software afterwards.

Deliberately uncorrected

The frames bypass the raster, the calibration and the skin transform by design. Nothing here is corrected or scaled, because a picture that lands where it should needs no correction afterwards. While the tool is up, nothing else is allowed to correct the screen either.

It is reachable from two places — the Screen & Aspect Ratio page, and the existing Calibration group beside Overscan alignment (which is what video calibration is now called, since it is per-mode overscan compensation rather than calibration in the colourimetric sense). The tool is also fully drivable over JSON-RPC, so a masking installer can program presets without touching the remote — see GUI.SetScreenAlignment.

09For skinners

Six new infolabels, available under both VideoPlayer. (what is playing) and ListItem. (an item in a list). They sit beside the existing VideoAspect, which continues to report the coded frame — the new labels report the content.

InfolabelReports
ContentAspectThe dominant ratio, as a number. ContentAspect(n) indexes the sections; ContentAspect(0) equals ContentAspect.
ContentAspectNameWhat that ratio is called — Scope, Todd-AO. Empty when it has no established name.
ContentAspectCountHow many distinct ratios the title was measured in. Zero means nothing was measured.
ContentAspectSourceOne of container, cached, live, declared.
ContentAspectVariesA boolean condition — true when the title changes shape partway through.
Why the count matters

A count of 0 beside a container source is the difference between "this title is 4:3" and "we have never looked". Two episodes from the same folder can legitimately report a measured 1.33 and an unmeasured 1.33, and only the count separates them.

Declaring a surround from the skin

A skin can state what should be drawn around the menus when they keep their own shape, via its xbmc.gui.skin extension point. This is what Skin default resolves to; the viewer's explicit choice always overrides it. 

<extension point="xbmc.gui.skin">
  <surround colour="FF101418" image="backdrop.jpg"/>
</extension>

Per-shape layouts already work

Skin resolution variants are selected against the raster, not the display. With a 2.40 screen shape stated, Skin.AspectRatio reports 21:9 and Estuary's existing 2560×1080 layout is selected and fills the band undistorted — a wide layout the skin already declares but which could never previously win, because selection targeted the display.

10For add-ons

Two new classes in the xbmc module, reached from either the player or a library item. Both are snapshots — they are copies, so a held object does not change underneath you when the geometry moves.

Obtained fromReturns
xbmc.Player().getContentGeometry()What is playing, or the shape Kodi rests at when nothing is.
InfoTagVideo.getContentGeometry()A library item's stored geometry.

ContentGeometry

getX() getY() getWidth() getHeight() — the picture within the frame. getFrameWidth() getFrameHeight() getCodedWidth() getCodedHeight() getPixelAspect() getOrientation() — the frame it sits in. getAspect() getLabel() getName() getSource() getVaries() getRejected() getStale() getSections().

ContentGeometrySection

One entry from getSections(), for a title that changes shape partway through: getX() getY() getWidth() getHeight() getAspect() getLabel() getName(). 

import xbmc

geometry = xbmc.Player().getContentGeometry()

# 'container' means nothing was measured - the frame is standing in for an answer
if geometry.getSource() != 'container':
    lens.select(geometry.getAspect())

if geometry.getVaries():
    for section in geometry.getSections():
        print(section.getAspect(), section.getName())
Deliberate departure

getContentGeometry() does not raise when nothing is playing, unlike every other info tag getter beside it. The resting shape is what an automation acts on between films, so it has to be readable then. getFrameWidth() is what separates "nothing playing" from "nothing measured".

11For automation

This is the surface a masking controller, a lens automation or a room processor actually drives. It is a JSON-RPC 14.0.0 addition.

Reading

MethodDoes
Player.GetGeometryThe overrides in force, the OSD placement, and screen — where the picture and the raster actually are, in screen pixels.
Player.GetDeclaredAspectRatioThe ratio declared for what is playing, if any.
GUI.GetScreenAlignmentThe alignment frames' state, and every ratio one can be drawn for.

Content geometry also rides on the existing item surfaces as Video.ContentRect — inside currentvideostream on the player, and on library items. There is no top-level contentrect player property.

Writing

MethodDoes
Player.SetGeometryState the raster, a maintained ratio, and the OSD placement for this playback.
Player.SetDeclaredAspectRatioOverride detection for what is playing. "auto" withdraws it.
GUI.SetScreenAlignmentShow or hide the alignment frames and choose which ratios are drawn.
VideoLibrary.RefreshContentGeometryRe-measure one file, replacing what is stored. Takes thorough for a far denser sample.
Override semantics

Every field of Player.SetGeometry is independent. State only the ones you are driving and the viewer's own choices stand for the rest. A field omitted, or given as null, is left exactly as it is — so an automation moving the lens need not restate an OSD placement it set earlier. Overrides last for this playback only and revert when it ends; to withdraw one without ending playback, give 0 for a ratio or "setting" for the placement.

Being told

Player.OnContentGeometryChange fires when playback starts, when a confirmed change occurs partway through a title, when the picture moves on the screen, and when playback ends — the last carrying the geometry of the GUI, because that is then what is on the screen. It carries the rectangles themselves, so acting on it needs no follow-up call.

The picture moving is a change in its own right: an instruction can move it without changing what the content is, and a display mode switch or a resized window moves it with no instruction at all.

A worked exchange

// The room tells Kodi its screen is 2.40 for this film, and to keep the OSD in the picture.
{ "jsonrpc": "2.0", "id": 1, "method": "Player.SetGeometry",
  "params": { "playerid": 1,
              "geometry": { "raster": 2.40, "osdplacement": "picture" } } }

// Kodi answers with where things actually ended up.
{ "jsonrpc": "2.0", "id": 1,
  "result": { "raster": 2.40,
              "osdplacement": "picture",
              "screen": {
                "picture": { "x": 0,   "y": 187, "width": 2560, "height": 1066 },
                "raster":  { "x": 0,   "y": 187, "width": 2560, "height": 1066 } } } }
picture vs raster

picture is the lit rectangle — what a mask is driven to. raster is the area Kodi is operating in — what a lens is driven to. They coincide when the content fills the room, and they do not whenever the content is narrower than it. Driving the wrong one is the single easiest mistake to make against this API.

12advancedsettings.xml

One tunable is deliberately not exposed in Settings, because the answer that suits a library is not the answer that suits one viewer's use of it. A room driving motorised masking wants to know about a sequence occupying a tenth of a film; a listing that simply names what a title is would rather not mention it. 

<advancedsettings>
  <video>
    <!-- share of a title's samples a second shape needs before it counts as varying -->
    <contentgeometryvariesshare>0.10</contentgeometryvariesshare>
  </video>
</advancedsettings>

Judged on sample counts rather than on confidence weight.

13Known limitations

These are known, and worth knowing before you build a room around the feature.

LimitationDetail
Video ignores per-mode overscan calibration With a non-default calibration and a raster stated, the interface honours the calibrated area but the picture is drawn on the raw pixel grid — so the picture can extend past the stated screen edge, onto the masking. It only arises where overscan calibration has been moved away from its defaults and a screen shape is stated.
Skin variants do not re-select on a display mode change A mode switch sends a resize but not a skin load, so the selected resolution variant can go stale until something else reloads the skin. Changing the screen shape corrects it immediately. Reloading the skin on every mode change would fix it, at a cost of its own.
An armed raster on an audio file The raster is applied at the first resolve against a real video stream, so a raster stated on Player.Open of an audio file is reported as in force without having reached the layout. Player.SetGeometry mid-playback covers the case.
Hardware decoding Live detection needs software-decoded frames, so it does not operate on a hardware-decoded stream. Scan-time measurement is unaffected.
Stereoscopic content Under 3D the reported geometry describes the packed frame, so a half-SBS clip reports a very wide ratio and gets labelled accordingly. The number and the label are both internally consistent, but they describe the packing rather than the film.

14Getting started

Nothing here is on by default, and a viewer who never opens the page sees Kodi behave exactly as it always has. This is the shortest path from that to seeing the feature work.

A five-minute path through it

  1. Open Settings › System › Screen & Aspect Ratio. Set the settings level to Advanced or higher, or none of it is visible.
  2. Turn on Detect content aspect ratio, then run Scan library for content aspect ratios and let it work through a few titles.
  3. Play a scope film. Check ContentAspect and ContentAspectName against the existing VideoAspect — the first two describe the film, the third describes the container.
  4. Set Screen aspect ratio to 2.40. The skin reloads, Estuary selects its wide layout, and the coded bars are cut rather than drawn.
  5. Play a title with IMAX sequences and watch ContentAspectCount read 2. Turn on Rescale the picture as its aspect ratio changes and watch the picture follow the boundaries.
  6. If you are driving a room, subscribe to Player.OnContentGeometryChange over the JSON-RPC TCP interface — the rectangles arrive with the notification, so there is no follow-up call to make.
If a title looks like the wrong shape

Check ContentAspectSource first. If it reads container, nothing was measured — which is a different problem from a measurement that came out wrong, and has a different answer. VideoLibrary.RefreshContentGeometry with thorough re-measures a single title far more densely than the scan does, which is what to reach for when a title cut in several ratios was recorded as having one shape.
